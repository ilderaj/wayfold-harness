# Artifact quality

Ground claims in available sources; distinguish facts, estimates, and proposals. Preserve dates, units, calculations, and uncertainty. Never turn internal or private evidence into public material without authorization. Use the Host's professional document/design tools; missing tools are a visible limitation.

Write plain, direct prose in the user's language. Preserve their voice; remove stock claims, decorative jargon, duplicated summaries, and unsupported certainty. Chinese technical writing should use consistent terminology and concrete actions. English should use familiar words without distorting meaning.

For UI/design, separate fixed requirements from proposed choices. Inspect the rendered artifact and representative interactions when execution is available; source checks alone do not establish usability. For office documents verify content, pagination/layout, tables, labels, and output format. Do not claim a visual review when none occurred.

Choose an explanation form that fits the question: short prose for a small fact; a diagram or interactive visual when relationships benefit. Cite actual code or evidence and label inferences. Personal templates are independent user assets, not bundled WFH requirements.
